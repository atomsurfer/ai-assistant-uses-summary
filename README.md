# Useful real-world AI assistant uses

A digest of replies to [Tim Ferriss](https://x.com/tferriss/status/2124313547773771817) asking, on November 21, 2026: “What's the most useful thing you've done with AI assistants/agents so far, even if mundane?” His own example was a 48-hour audit that found and cancelled old subscriptions across multiple cards, out-of-date excess insurance policies, and outdated employee services — about $10,000 a year in savings. The thread that followed is less about flashy demos than about audits, claims, paperwork, kids’ homework, meal planning, and other chores people actually finished.

## Finding money: subscriptions, insurance, taxes, and refunds

The most repeated pattern is a spending or policy audit with a human still approving the final action. Ferriss later spelled out his prompt: look for subscriptions he might not be using, or that had been going for more than 10 years; list the most expensive ones that totaled at least $500 a year; give the assistant read-only access to credit-card statements and the inbox; and have it draft cancellation emails for him to approve ([@tferriss](https://x.com/tferriss/status/2124334954947678691), [follow-up on permissions](https://x.com/tferriss/status/2124332265857827023)). Several people reported doing a similar purge, including forgotten renewals that had been written off as one-time charges ([@SteveKBark](https://x.com/SteveKBark/status/2124327551736950912), [@harden_369741](https://x.com/harden_369741/status/2124365968180748555), [@wenghiih7](https://x.com/wenghiih7/status/2124372341627179414)).

Concrete dollar outcomes showed up well beyond subscriptions:

- [@bentzysu](https://x.com/bentzysu/status/2124366140012933585) had Muse find a better mortgage and walk through who to call and what to say, saving $13,000 a year, or $91,000 over seven years.
- [@andysbozz](https://x.com/andysbozz/status/2124334098311229682) built a restaurant-supply price tool that was saving around $18,000 a year on broccoli, $7,000 on olive oil, and $25,000 on packaging. In a follow-up he described the setup: OpenAI Codex plus QuickBooks, daily vendor price tracking, and a search for equivalent suppliers (including a Greek olive-oil importer with a one-month minimum order) — built without being a software engineer ([@andysbozz](https://x.com/andysbozz/status/2124338611818574032)).
- [@BoredElonMusk](https://x.com/BoredElonMusk/status/2124324739565211964) used assistants after a house fire to push an insurer toward a full-limits payout, saving “hundreds of hours and hundreds of thousands of dollars.” The method: scan every insurer letter for errors and pushback points; audit a lowball rebuild estimate against current labor and material prices; and build a personal-property inventory by scanning photos into a spreadsheet of items, ages, and prices, with the model suggesting things they had forgotten ([@BoredElonMusk](https://x.com/BoredElonMusk/status/2124380936800477620)).
- [@NofONEjourney](https://x.com/NofONEjourney/status/2124367269560267193) audited their taxes, found a niche credit a CPA had missed, and got a $30,000 amended-return adjustment with three days to spare. [@TheGiftConvo](https://x.com/TheGiftConvo/status/2124385709046092158) said doing taxes with AI saved about $5,000.
- [@MiroC](https://x.com/MiroC/status/2124321240114663515) had an assistant find an old Air Canada cancellation text, read the regulations, file the form, and deal with the airline — a $1,000 refund two days later. [@LVarbee](https://x.com/LVarbee/status/2124341955236937735) used a bot to follow up after an airline declined compensation for delays over nine hours; two emails with a customer-service rep led to $2,000 in payment.
- [@rafaelcoutinho](https://x.com/rafaelcoutinho/status/2124443463160676527) had an agent haggle with a human insurance broker — anchoring on last year’s price, pulling market data, asking for alternatives, and following up on a manager’s discount — and cut the premium from R$4,085 to R$2,710 (34%) for the same coverage.
- [@gertieok](https://x.com/gertieok/status/2124327733912350733) listed a negotiated payoff that saved $3,500, along with IRS paperwork, apartment marketing that found a tenant, and other chores. [@JarredRobidouxx](https://x.com/JarredRobidouxx/status/2124499389016059932) rebuilt outdated payment software and moved a title company to Stripe, saving $3,000 a year (about $100 a year in upkeep, a 97% cost cut). [@ChaosBioVC](https://x.com/ChaosBioVC/status/2124323493575524444) got an $8.99 YouTube Premium refund after signing up on the wrong account.

A common caution, including from Ferriss, was to start read-only and keep cancellation or payment behind a person’s yes ([@tferriss](https://x.com/tferriss/status/2124338250114642181), [@thetiktokwiz](https://x.com/thetiktokwiz/status/2124484319259320349), [@Iammdshohag](https://x.com/Iammdshohag/status/2124469739816632331)).

## Business operations and automation

Replies from operators were less about chat and more about closing loops that used to take hours.

[@devilmaycare_9](https://x.com/devilmaycare_9/status/2124328666255794241) turned a 30-minute report pull, done multiple times a week by 40 people, into a 15-second task — roughly $200,000 a year in mindless hours. The old forms had five levels of drop-downs (hundreds of options each); the new flow needed three inputs and filled the rest automatically ([@devilmaycare_9](https://x.com/devilmaycare_9/status/2124374764194296311)). [@TimmyGack](https://x.com/TimmyGack/status/2124357955529199647) listed financial reporting that lands 90%+ faster with no hand-pulled numbers or Excel reconciliations; customer-service automations that save two hours a day and keep orders moving on snow days; plain-English business data questions over MCP instead of custom SQL or new Power BI reports; meeting tools that capture action items and have an agent chase follow-ups; and a CMS where adding a product in admin publishes prices, tax codes, and photos to Stripe. [@ohiondfan_](https://x.com/ohiondfan_/status/2124343714860736689) fully automated an accounts-payable process (Claude estimated 500 hours of labor a year). [@ksugarte](https://x.com/ksugarte/status/2124436267534569648) replaced a paper audit across 100+ bakeries and dropped wastage from above 5% to below 2%.

Other operational examples: [@solomonsgrave](https://x.com/solomonsgrave/status/2124364935203549596) posted a help-wanted ad, used Gmail, ElevenLabs, and a rented number so an agent could screen applicants in Hindi, and hired a driver on day three. [@thiruvenkadamrs](https://x.com/thiruvenkadamrs/status/2124481220381905176) described agents at Meion Labs that run a hospital insurance desk in India — reading documents, filling pre-authorization forms, filing on each insurer’s portal, and tracking claims. [@suly_ben](https://x.com/suly_ben/status/2124342491604480316) said agents now handle daily outreach, lead follow-up, and job quotes with minimal supervision, generating $10,000 in the first month in a domain they already knew. [@BowTiedHamilton](https://x.com/BowTiedHamilton/status/2124487347563868168) built an “office staff layer” for a small business and avoided a hire. [@TheInnovatorAi](https://x.com/TheInnovatorAi/status/2124395146758201850) uses an agent in real estate to chase documents, flag the contract clause that matters, and draft follow-ups so deals don’t die in an inbox. [@brian_lambert](https://x.com/brian_lambert/status/2124337470540288314) uses Grok bots to help manage a $2 million-a-month Meta media-buying budget. [@kpitessential](https://x.com/kpitessential/status/2124353141282095239) has a daily report on what wedding-magazine advertisers posted, plus monitoring of bridal brands and celebrity weddings; Grok also caught a long-standing phone-number discrepancy in proofreading ([@kpitessential](https://x.com/kpitessential/status/2124353641217765884)).

Smaller but repeatable office wins included snapping receipt photos into a company expense-report template ([@zz7373zz7373](https://x.com/zz7373zz7373/status/2124314161366909087)), converting PDFs to Excel and catching spreadsheet errors ([@Lanalyzethis](https://x.com/Lanalyzethis/status/2124317461676003339)), sorting years of receipts in a weekend ([@beth2049_](https://x.com/beth2049_/status/2124314169092579646)), drafting firm emails to clients who went quiet ([@gabriel_va91354](https://x.com/gabriel_va91354/status/2124326580407144741)), turning a year of customer emails into a list of questions the website never answers ([@iguptapankaj](https://x.com/iguptapankaj/status/2124335013277941944)), a pull-request and incident review agent with read-only access and a checklist ([@senthazalravi](https://x.com/senthazalravi/status/2124438852001464626)), and pressure-testing a proposed new business location for shaky growth assumptions and downside scenarios ([@BrianEasonLead](https://x.com/BrianEasonLead/status/2124315492399632462)). [@mkdbuilds](https://x.com/mkdbuilds/status/2124399718549565913) has patients fill in information before a visit, then uses AI to draft reports and certificates for a doctor to check. [@arjanjohan](https://x.com/arjanjohan/status/2124385189757374572) automated hours tracking across clients for tax authorities.

A few people also pointed at self-running content or research systems: [@TopherNOW](https://x.com/TopherNOW/status/2124326474735845805) built a free searchable database of publicly announced commercial real-estate transactions, with bots that find deals, draft and QA pages, and publish trend videos (85,000 video views, 2,000 website hits, and 80+ newsletter signups in 30 days). [@hlord1408](https://x.com/hlord1408/status/2124395604772266329) has an agent refresh an online contact directory weekly; it gets 8,000 visitors a month. [@kel_mccall](https://x.com/kel_mccall/status/2124314875602350517) built a Reddit bot that pulls three relevant trending posts each morning and drafts replies from a coaching database.

## Learning, kids, and knowledge

The standout education example was custom practice, not generic tutoring. [@VenessaGilbride](https://x.com/VenessaGilbride/status/2124393306503499806) built an agent for a second grader who struggled with math facts: snap a photo of the week’s homework, weave in the facts being practiced, and track every fact toward a goal of all facts to 20 by the end of second grade. Two weeks in, the child knew all the doubles, and the teacher said to skip the class homework and do this instead. [@jboonecpa](https://x.com/jboonecpa/status/2124489612970369352) has been making practice quizzes for a third grader from the teacher’s study guides and is considering a dedicated study bot.

Adults described similar “tell me what to study, then I study” setups: a career-changer’s bot that keeps a daily study schedule and materials ([@FelisCalculata](https://x.com/FelisCalculata/status/2124341752639234302)); a Gemini protocol for conversational French ([@BWellard99](https://x.com/BWellard99/status/2124326047089041625)); a language app for vocab, conjugations, grammar, writing, and speaking ([@Elfiny](https://x.com/Elfiny/status/2124462139490357621)); flowcharts generated from textbooks, articles, and podcast scripts before diving in ([@Oliver_Morina_](https://x.com/Oliver_Morina_/status/2124321597590819217)); sketchnotes of learning materials ([@HarryRamstrong](https://x.com/HarryRamstrong/status/2124377401127800968)); Anki cards from meetup notes ([@cupaobaidou](https://x.com/cupaobaidou/status/2124324147279040599)); and a weekly scan for fellowships and internships ([@prottushx](https://x.com/prottushx/status/2124431479707218100)).

For a personal knowledge base, [@tiger26297](https://x.com/tiger26297/status/2124384407780040932) fed 15 years of bookmarks and 20 years of ebooks and course PDFs into a “second brain” wiki with a Claude-powered ask interface that can also build tutorials from the archived material. [@tmr_erg](https://x.com/tmr_erg/status/2124329851951960081) files notes and backlinks across areas of life with Claude. [@thomoliverz](https://x.com/thomoliverz/status/2124457894045200445) built a product that packages unread articles, URLs, and files into an email or PDF on a chosen schedule. [@AnthonyKoochew](https://x.com/AnthonyKoochew/status/2124364136197083194) turns research papers into a long-form notebook podcast. [@jonwalz](https://x.com/jonwalz/status/2124372067063889949) used it with a daughter to make videos on the lives of the Saints.

## Home, family, and everyday admin

Neighborhood and family logistics got some of the strongest engagement. [@mar15sa](https://x.com/mar15sa/status/2124330983529058733) built a neighborhood map of local makers, parks, trails, and what’s being built; it started as a personal project and now has more than 2,000 neighbors using it every month. The build used Three.js, real elevation data, OpenStreetMap roads, trail maps, image-generated landmarks, and an automated pipeline that adds helpful local info (for example, registration forms for an elementary school opening) and shares the useful bits on socials ([@mar15sa](https://x.com/mar15sa/status/2124352845998879224)). [@nemorikki](https://x.com/nemorikki/status/2124321179729101013) has a weekly agent search for events in a given radius and assemble a weekend list. [@dominicbressan](https://x.com/dominicbressan/status/2124462274710757565) built an interactive master map of a 140-site residential and tourism community, with filters for layouts, rental terms, power and water, and repairs.

[@TonyChanBuilds](https://x.com/TonyChanBuilds/status/2124320964557345086), a single dad of two girls, has agents keep the calendar around school days, catch school events in email, find cheap weekend outings, and plan Thanksgiving week and Black Friday shopping. [@Samhuze](https://x.com/Samhuze/status/2124335613889618014) auto-sends a daily, age- and season-appropriate chore list to a printer for the kids, starring chores that come with pay.

Meal planning was one of the most copied mundane wins. [@jdmullin](https://x.com/jdmullin/status/2124368969578823949) sits down with Claude once a week: it reads the family calendar, plans around games and events (15-minute or make-ahead meals on busy nights, no spicy food for a spouse, no repeats the kids are sick of), drops recipes into Paprika, then an MCP server fills a Fred Meyer cart with remembered brands, sizes, UPCs, and 18 weekly staples. Planning that used to take a couple of hours is now about 15 minutes. Similar grocery and meal setups came from [@ryanlanciaux](https://x.com/ryanlanciaux/status/2124319509687484436), [@hirstmusic](https://x.com/hirstmusic/status/2124358164124401931), and [@thejeffcallahan](https://x.com/thejeffcallahan/status/2124315583093334473).

## Top 10 most actionable

1. **Run a subscription and vendor audit with a dollar floor.** Ask for unused charges or anything running more than ~10 years, and only surface items that add up to a meaningful annual amount (Ferriss used $500+). Start with read-only statement and inbox access; draft cancellations, then approve them yourself ([@tferriss](https://x.com/tferriss/status/2124334954947678691)).
2. **Turn invoices into a purchasing assistant.** Feed a year of invoices and current vendors into a model, track prices, and search for equivalent suppliers that meet the same specs — the restaurant example was tens of thousands of dollars a year ([@andysbozz](https://x.com/andysbozz/status/2124338611818574032)).
3. **Treat an insurance dispute like a document-review project.** Scan every letter for errors, compare rebuild or settlement numbers to current market prices, and build a property inventory from photos, including items you forgot ([@BoredElonMusk](https://x.com/BoredElonMusk/status/2124380936800477620)).
4. **Revisit old travel disruptions.** Search email or texts for cancelled flights or long delays, check the actual regulations, and have the assistant draft or file the claim ([@MiroC](https://x.com/MiroC/status/2124321240114663515), [@LVarbee](https://x.com/LVarbee/status/2124341955236937735)).
5. **Audit a tax return for missed credits before the window closes.** One reply found a niche credit a CPA missed and amended for $30,000 with three days left ([@NofONEjourney](https://x.com/NofONEjourney/status/2124367269560267193)).
6. **Shop a mortgage (or an insurance premium) with a script.** Have the assistant find a better offer and tell you who to call and what to say, or negotiate with a broker against last year’s price and market comps ([@bentzysu](https://x.com/bentzysu/status/2124366140012933585), [@rafaelcoutinho](https://x.com/rafaelcoutinho/status/2124443463160676527)).
7. **Clean one bloated inbox or photo library with a filing scheme and filters.** Examples ranged from 50,000+ emails labeled and cleared, to property records pulled out of Gmail in about four hours, to thousands of photos sorted by date and subject ([@SteveKBark](https://x.com/SteveKBark/status/2124327551736950912), [@VipanGoel](https://x.com/VipanGoel/status/2124507292683141272), [@Nichole4887822](https://x.com/Nichole4887822/status/2124498539996672463)).
8. **Automate family meals around the real calendar.** Read the week’s events, plan around constraints, drop recipes into a recipe app, and optionally fill a grocery cart with remembered brands — then just review and check out ([@jdmullin](https://x.com/jdmullin/status/2124368969578823949)).
9. **Make homework from the actual worksheet.** Photograph whatever came home from school, generate practice that matches this week’s material, and track the facts or skills over time ([@VenessaGilbride](https://x.com/VenessaGilbride/status/2124393306503499806), [@jboonecpa](https://x.com/jboonecpa/status/2124489612970369352)).
10. **Collapse a repetitive report or form into a few inputs.** If 40 people are clicking the same five-level drop-downs, keep three required fields and let the assistant fill the rest ([@devilmaycare_9](https://x.com/devilmaycare_9/status/2124374764194296311)). A close cousin is a neighborhood or local-events map that started as a personal tool and became useful to thousands of neighbors ([@mar15sa](https://x.com/mar15sa/status/2124330983529058733), [@nemorikki](https://x.com/nemorikki/status/2124321179729101013)).
